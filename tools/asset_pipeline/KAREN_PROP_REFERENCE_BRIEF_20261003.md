## Evidence and limits

1. SAC vernacular-house record, Pgakenyaw, Pang Hin Fon, Mae Chaem, Chiang Mai: https://thai-vernacular-houses.sac.or.th/en/thai-vernacular-houses-detail.php?ob_id=21
   The record identifies a stilt house with timber/bamboo walls and hip roofing. Drawings show a raised main volume, open undercroft, access stairs and an attached platform. Its mixed natural/zinc roof is a specific house, not a universal ethnic template.
2. SAC Ban Huai Hee community record: https://wikicommunity.sac.or.th/community/1006
   Housing description reports timber structure, split-bamboo walls, and pine shingles or tong-ko leaf roofing. A photographed building shows bamboo panels, exposed supports, an open lower area and contemporary metal roofing. The traditional game design may use natural roofing; do not claim all present-day homes do.
3. IMPECT Pgakenyaw cultural account: https://impect.or.th/15026/
   Additional reference for bamboo construction and everyday veranda uses.
4. KPRU local community study, Khlong Lan Pgakenyaw: https://arit.kpru.ac.th/ap/local/?code_db=610004&code_type=05&nu=pages&page_id=2099
   Describes separate grain storage and locality-specific placement customs. This is a different locality; do not apply its siting rules to every Karen community without further evidence.
5. Photographed Ban Pha Mon Pgakenyaw rice bank and community interview: https://mgronline.com/onlinesection/detail/9590000114830
   Inspected photograph saved as pgakenyaw_rice_bank.jpg: raised enclosed storage volume, timber frame, bamboo wall panels, access ladder, simple pitched roof. The photo uses contemporary metal roofing; our natural leaf roof is an explicit game adaptation required by the manifest, not a verified replica. The prompt omits the photo's signboard and surroundings.
6. Somjaimak and Saicharoent, 2019, vernacular-house field study in the Mae Chaem headwaters: https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/arch-kku/article/download/143320/152208/
   Field research supports variation in buildings, materials and separate grain-storage structures. Retained PDF and inspected layout figure provide context; avoid universal claims about one arrangement.

Research images and drawings are retained under artifacts/meshy_props_20261003/references for inspection only; they are not shipping textures or website media. Filename labels are descriptive placeholders: the Pang Hin Fon files are architectural drawings, not photographs. ban_huai_hee.jpg is the inspected photograph.

## Generation and review constraints

- Start from researched construction: raised timber frame, legible split/woven bamboo wall panels, practical steps and veranda, open undercroft, restrained roof masses.
- Distinguish variants through plan, veranda position and roof construction supported by references.
- Do not invent decorative ethnic motifs, temple finials, unrelated Lanna ornament, or use a generic 'tribal' prompt.
- Use muted timber, bamboo and natural roof colours. Visible angular facets, broad material regions, no photorealistic noise, glossy surfaces or baked illumination.
- Preserve doors, stairs and gaps below floors. Reject melted/fused supports, blocked openings and unsupported roof details.
- Review silhouette and four views, then actual game distance. Cultural reference evidence is necessary but does not substitute for community review.
- Normalize and validate using the existing SOP; preserve rollback originals. Install only accepted candidates and run tests/test_all.gd after each installed batch. Do not edit scripts/, ui/ or tests/test_all.gd.
